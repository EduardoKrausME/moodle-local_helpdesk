TICKETS AND CONVERSATIONS

From the first question
to completed support.

A ticket starts with a subject, category, description and priority. The editor lets the user explain the situation clearly, while attachments help share screenshots and documents. The opening and reply forms accept up to five files per submission, subject to the environment limits.

Support staff can select a user when creating the request, allowing them to register support on behalf of the person who needs help. When the ticket is associated with a course, that context follows the request.

Reply and define the next step

You can reply, reply and mark as resolved, or reply and close. Changes to category, priority and status are recorded in the history, helping everyone understand how the support process evolved.

CATEGORIES AND TEAMS

Organize support
the way your institution works.

Create categories with a name and description to separate subjects and define who is responsible for support. Registrar, technical support and academic support are examples of how this structure can reflect your institution's reality.

For each category, select the users who may reply and who should receive emails about new tickets. Category management and ticket handling have separate permissions, allowing roles to be designed with different responsibilities.

People asking for help see their own tickets

Users without management permission can view their own requests. For support staff, user, course, category, status and priority filters help find the desired ticket. Text search considers the subject and description starting at three characters.

OPTIONAL GENIAI INTEGRATION

More help with writing.
The team stays in control of the reply.

GeniAI helps create replies directly inside the support workflow. Support staff describe what they want to communicate and receive a text suggestion that can be sent to the editor, reviewed and adjusted before replying to the user.

Generation considers the subject, ticket description and, when available, up to the three most recent messages in the conversation. This lets the suggestion take the immediate support context into account.

The same integration also helps draft knowledge-base articles from a description supplied by the team. The feature depends on GeniAI being installed and configured with its API key.

NOTIFICATIONS AND AUTOMATED ROUTINES

A ticket does not have to
be forgotten.

Email communication follows ticket opening and replies. Two scheduled tasks complement the team's routine using the information recorded in each ticket.

3 days

A reminder for the team

Open or in-progress tickets created at least three days ago and still without a first response can generate a reminder for the people responsible for the category. The plugin records the notification so the same reminder is not repeated for that ticket.

48 hours

Closure after resolution

Tickets that remain in resolved status for at least 48 hours are closed by the automated task. The period is counted from the recorded resolution time.

✉

Notifications with context

Emails include ticket references, subject, category and message, plus attachments when applicable. Links take the user directly to the ticket to make the conversation easier to continue.

Both tasks are configured to run daily at midnight and depend on the environment cron. Deadlines are checked when the task runs; they do not mean an email will be sent or a ticket closed at the exact minute.

REPORTS WITH KOPERE BI

Understand demand.
Monitor support.

The plugin report files organize queries into five groups. See what each analysis can reveal and how this information can support service management.

TICKETSOpening and resolution over time+

Tickets created by period. Groups openings by category and creation day, showing the recorded quantity. It helps identify peaks in demand on specific dates.

Tickets resolved by period. The query uses the closure date, grouped by month and category, to count closed tickets. This distinction is important for interpreting the displayed indicator correctly.

CATEGORIESWhere demand is concentrated+

Shows the total number of tickets in each category, ordered from the highest volume. It includes average first-response time and the average time between opening and closure for records that contain those dates.

This view helps identify recurring subjects and support groups that deserve closer analysis.

STATUS AND PRIORITYDistribution and support times+

Tickets by status. Combines category and status to show volume, average first response and average closure time.

Distribution by priority. Groups tickets by priority and shows the same time measurements. This helps reveal whether urgent requests are receiving the expected attention.

USERSWho requests support most often+

Lists the ten users with the highest number of tickets, including identification, email, total requests, and average first-response and closure times.

The query considers tickets registered by the user without limiting the count to tickets that are still open. The result can help identify recurring guidance needs.

TEAM ACTIVITYParticipation in replies+

The report called “Tickets by agent” in the plugin counts records in the replies table, grouped by the user who created them. Its meaning is therefore the number of reply records, not the number of unique tickets assigned.

Use this information as support when analyzing participation in conversations, always considering the context of each support interaction.

Description based on the report files in the reviewed version. Queries use six-hour or one-day cache depending on the report and should not be interpreted as real-time indicators.

EXPORT TO EXCEL

Take the history
into your own analysis.

Export tickets to an Excel spreadsheet with the user's name, course, subject, description, status, priority, category and email. Replies are added in separate columns with identification of the person who wrote each message.

The export lets you choose ticket statuses and respects the course when that context is provided. Textual content is converted to plain text, making it easier to read and process outside the environment.

The feature requires ticket-management permission. The export has its own status selection and starts with open tickets by default.

BEFORE YOU START

Some frequently asked questions.

Does Helpdesk work outside Moodle™ software?+

It is a local plugin installed in the environment and uses its accounts, courses and permissions. This page presents the product; it is not a support center connected to your institution.

Is artificial intelligence required?+

No. GeniAI is optional. Ticket creation, team replies and the other support features do not depend on AI text generation.

Does AI send the reply automatically?+

The reviewed workflow generates a suggestion that the team can transfer to the editor. The person responsible can review the text and choose the reply action in the form.

What is the difference between resolved and closed?+

They are separate statuses. Resolved means the solution has been provided; closed ends the cycle. The daily task closes tickets that have remained resolved for at least 48 hours.

Can I separate support by course?+

The plugin accepts course context, provides a course filter in the ticket view and considers that context in export. It also lets you configure the menu to appear only inside courses.
